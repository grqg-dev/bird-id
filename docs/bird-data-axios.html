Bird-ID · backyard acoustics  //  Smart Brevity edition

A Santa Barbara mic logged 373 bird calls in under an hour

Driving the news: A passive microphone on Glendessary Lane ran for four short windows on May 30 and BirdNET tagged 373 detections across 11 species — from a relentless crow to a single, unsure hummingbird.

  • Why it matters: Cheap always-on audio + a free neural net turns one backyard into a biodiversity sensor — no binoculars, no expert required.
  • By the numbers: 57.6 minutes of tape · 4 recording windows · top species American Crow at 126 detections (34% of everything).
  • The catch: "Detections" aren't birds, and the model's confidence is left fully visible below — including where it's clearly guessing.

⚠ Reality check

  1. Recording is discontinuous. Four windows = 57.6 min; the 5h38m gap between dawn and afternoon is unrecorded, not quiet.
  2. Counts are calls, not birds. 126 crow detections can be one loud crow near the mic firing over and over.
  3. Confidence ≠ correctness. It's BirdNET's raw score, and the left edge is clipped at min_conf 0.30 — the pileup there is the setting, not the bird.
  4. Two IDs are shaky. Spotted Towhee tops out at 0.565 over 23 hits; Anna's Hummingbird is one 0.388. Treat as unconfirmed.
  5. One day, one yard. "Morning vs afternoon" = which recording, not bird behavior. Don't generalize from n = 1 day.
